# Literature Benchmark Results

## Scope

These are **reported results from the studies reviewed in the submitted paper**, not new measurements generated by this repository.

The paper's Table 1 shows very wide variation in reported fabrication/hallucination rates. Examples include 55% for GPT-3.5 versus 18% for GPT-4 in Walters & Wilder, 19.9% for GPT-4o in Linardon et al., 39.8% erroneous/fabricated references across eight chatbots in Cabezas-Clavijo & Sidorenko-Bautista, 17–33% hallucination in the legal RAG tools evaluated by Magesh et al., and 14.23–94.93% across 13 LLMs in GhostCite.

## Why the rates cannot simply be averaged

The studies differ in:

- model/version;
- prompt;
- domain;
- sample size;
- definition of fabrication;
- whether a DOI error counts as fabrication;
- whether claim-level citation support is measured;
- closed-book vs retrieval-grounded operation.

Therefore the chart in `figures/literature_benchmark_rates.png` is a **visual literature map**, not a statistically pooled meta-analysis.

## Central interpretation

The evidence in the submitted paper supports three conclusions:

1. Citation fabrication is persistent across generations and system types.
2. Retrieval grounding can reduce fabrication, but it does not eliminate citation errors or claim misgrounding.
3. Agentic systems add retrieval, ranking, crawler-access and URL-health failure modes, so a single end-to-end fabrication percentage can hide multiple causal stages.

## Reproducible extension

A future primary experiment should use identical prompts across selected LLMs and agents, save raw outputs, parse references, verify every reference against authoritative metadata, and score existence, bibliographic accuracy, URL validity and claim-level support separately.
